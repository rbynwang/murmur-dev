A year of evenings: what the community promised, and what was delivered

48 chosen evenings of ENGIE's 2025, 12 in each season, for one community: 200 homes, a small office and shops, with its homes' batteries held for the peak. Each evening was run with code plans at three tiers of equipment (tier 1: the full device mix; tier 2: water heaters; tier 3: no added devices), on ENGIE's own weather and on the real day-to-day weather of 2025, with the community agent and without it. The truth is what the simulated devices did. The buyers' meter is what the buyers measured and paid on.

With the community agent and without it

The community agent does not change what is delivered or what members are paid. It changes the promise. Without it the community promises everything its members offer and truly falls short on 48 of 48 evenings at tier 1. With it the true shortfall is 2.7% in place of 16.9%, about 6 times smaller.

A better baseline: a first look

The baseline is the buyers' estimate of what the community would have drawn with no sale. Delivery, pay and whether a promise was kept all rest on it, and today it is a rule. A line fitted on what is known before 18:00 cuts the mean error from 7.6% to 5.5% on ENGIE's weather and from 11.6% to 7.3% on real weather. Every candidate is judged only on evenings it was not fitted on, one month left out at a time.

This is a first look, not a trained product. 252 evenings a version is little, only one form of model was tried, it was tried for the whole community at tier 1 only, and nothing in the buyers' code was changed.

What is not real. Devices and meters are simulated. The buyers are stand-ins with fixed orders, and in the NANDA Town evenings a stand-in takes the place of the aggregator's own agent. The homes' batteries are replayed from ENGIE's record as one total. No data center is in these runs.

Every number on this page is read from the runs' own result files by the script that wrote the page.
