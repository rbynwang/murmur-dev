How Murmur simulates a community, its homes and its devices

ENGIE's dataset gives a community as one total at its meter. Everything inside that total is simulated by us, built from public measured data and checked against it.

1

The community

ENGIE's total, opened into parts.

The host is one of the 20 communities in ENGIE's dataset (COM_19 in the files). Its record stays as ENGIE gave it, for all of 2025 in 15-minute steps. Our homes and buildings are placed inside it.

Taken from ENGIE, unchanged

  • ENGIENet power at the community's meter, every 15 minutes
  • ENGIEIts homes' batteries, added up: 1,624 kW and 4,400 kWh, with their reported state of charge
  • ENGIESolar size 3,416 kW, connection limit 3,214 kW
  • ENGIETemperature, sunlight and real-time prices

How the total is opened

use = solar + battery discharge − net poweruse = 200 homes + office + shops + other use

  • ModelLine one turns ENGIE's net power into use. ENGIE does not report a community's solar, so by day it is estimated from ENGIE's sunlight with a rule fitted on ENGIE's 35 metered solar plants (R² 0.92). After dark there is no solar to estimate.
  • ModelLine two places our parts. Other use is what is left of ENGIE's record. Nobody controls it.

Does it fit?

  • ModelFrom 18:00 to 21:00, the hours of every sale, it fits in all 4,380 quarter hours of the year. Other use never falls below 584 kW.
  • ModelOver all hours it fits in 94% of quarter hours. In the other 6%, around midnight when our cars charge and around midday when solar is an estimate, our parts exceed ENGIE's record and other use is held at zero.
  • ModelOver the whole year our homes are 23% of the community's use, the office 3% and the shops 6%. Other use is 68%.
  • ENGIEWeather is ENGIE's own. Its temperature changes 0.27 °C from day to day, where measured Massachusetts weather changes 2.6 °C. So our 200 homes vary 4 to 6% between evenings here, against 7 to 13% on real weather. A second version of the year carries real day-to-day movement.

How one evening runs

  1. RequestThe aggregator asks for less draw from 18:00 to 21:00. The year has 48 such evenings, four a month, chosen by rule before any run.
  2. OffersEach home and building agent offers what it can give, from its own forecast.
  3. PromiseThe community agent adds the offers, holds back a cover learned from earlier evenings, promises the rest and holds its members' batteries for the peak.
  4. Devices actAgents plan again every 15 minutes. A guard checks every 5-minute part before it reaches a device.
  5. Measured twiceBy the meter, against a baseline: the ten most recent similar days, adjusted to the day itself. By the truth, against a twin of each home and building run with no sale.

All 200 homes and both buildings run every evening offline, at three equipment levels. On a sample of those evenings, eight homes, both buildings, the community agent and the aggregator's agent also run as separate programs that exchange signed messages over NANDA Town.

2

Each home and household

A physics model with people in it.

Each home runs in a house simulator (homesim, from MassHack, extended by us). The house is one thermal mass. Its furnace, heat pump or air conditioner holds the thermostat against the outdoor temperature and the sun. People, appliances, the car and the roof sit on top.

Three equipment levels, same 200 homes

  • ModelLeast. The newest solar and a car on a smart charger.
  • ModelMiddle. Adds a home battery, a smart thermostat and a heat pump water heater.
  • AssumedFull. Adds the larger smart appliances and a two-way car. That car is an ideal case: 34 of the 200 cars are models that can power a home today.

What we set ourselves

  • AssumedEach owner's limits: how much warmer the house may get (0.8 to 3.0 °C), a car timer (118 homes), devices that are off limits (189 owners).
  • AssumedThe homes are Massachusetts house types placed in ENGIE's community. Heating and cooling are worked out again on ENGIE's weather.

The control loop

  1. Plan, every 15 minutes
  2. Three 5-minute parts
  3. Guard
  4. Device

While a home owes something, a small linear program plans the battery, the car and the cooling again every 15 minutes. It weighs the shortfall, new peaks around the window, and discomfort. It can't break the battery's reserve or leave a car short at departure, and the guard holds the house inside the owner's temperature limit.

A twin of the same home runs beside it with no sale. The gap between the two meters is the truth we score against.

One road to a device

  1. Agent
  2. Tool
  3. Controller
  4. Guard
  5. Driver
  6. Device

The guard seals the exact values it approved, and a driver accepts nothing else. Real equipment needs only a different driver. The rest of the code is the same.

Code decides every amount. The language model's part is words: it reads an owner's note, such as a wish for a full battery tonight, and turns it into limits the code must keep.

In a building

A building runs the same loop with a building's levers. We built an office, a shopping center and a university campus. ENGIE's community holds a small office and a strip of shops, each at 4% of the full model. The office averages 39 kW with a peak of 58. The shops average 81 kW with a peak of 156.

LeverThe limit the guard keeps
Zone cooling: run warmer, or pre-coolAt most 1.5 °C warmer than the building's own day while open, 3 °C while closed.
Air handler fans: cap the speedNever stopped. Free zones slow to half flow, the code's minimum.
Zone standby: a free zone cuts air and coolingNot while open if any room lacks a sensor.
Cooling plant: lower its limit, or stop it earlyOnly a plant cleared to stop. Never one that serves labs.
Lights: dim a groupAt most 25% in the shops, 50% in the office. Never stairs or exits.
Plug loads: switch a group offEmpty rooms only. Never network or security.

Each building is a set of zones with an occupancy plan made the day before and the day as it truly went. Kitchen hoods, garage fans and food refrigeration are watched, never switched.

What is real

The four sources, gathered in one place.

ENGIE

Unchanged

  • The community's meter, battery, solar size and limit
  • Weather and prices
  • Other use: the 68% of the community's year we leave as it is
Measured

Public data we build on

  • National Grid's average home and business classes
  • A measured multi-story instrumented research house dataset, circuit-level heat pump, lighting, and plug loads
  • Boston Public Library's meter, Boston's building reports
  • The federal household survey, makers' datasheets
Model

Computed

  • Each house's heating and cooling
  • Every device's power and limits
  • The community's solar by day
  • The agents' plans and the guard's checks
Assumed

Set by us

  • Car driving and charging habits. No public measured home-charging data exists.
  • Household types and owners' limits
  • A two-way car in every home at the full level
  • How a building's loads split among zones

No real home, meter or device is connected. A command travels the road it would travel to real equipment, and only the last step, the driver, is simulated.
